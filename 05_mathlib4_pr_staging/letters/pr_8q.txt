In the previous push, we showed that for any prime number p greater than the finite cutoff N, the discrete Nyman cell residual jumps by a fixed amount c 1 (which is -1 under the Dirichlet anchor) when we move from cell p - 1 to cell p. That qualitative jump was enough to prove that the L2 integral on (0, 1] cannot be zero, but it did not give an explicit formula for how much L2 energy each prime p forces into the sum.

This single push closes that gap by giving an exact algebraic formula for the combined L2 energy of the two adjacent cells p - 1 and p. Even when we do not know the individual cell values a = discreteCellResidual c N (p - 1) and b = discreteCellResidual c N p, we know their difference b - a = c 1. Because the width of cell p - 1 is 1 / ((p - 1) * p) and the width of cell p is 1 / (p * (p + 1)), their two denominators (p - 1) * p = p^2 - p and p * (p + 1) = p^2 + p add up to 2 * p^2, where the -p and +p cancel out completely. Using this nice cancellation, we complete the square by basic school algebra and prove that every prime p > N contributes at least (c 1)^2 / (2 * p^2), which is 1 / (2 * p^2) when c 1 = -1.

The step by step maths behind how this works is very simple:

Step 1: Let a be the constant residual value on cell p - 1 of width 1 / ((p - 1) * p), and let b be the constant residual value on cell p of width 1 / (p * (p + 1)). The combined L2 energy of these two adjacent cells is
E = a^2 / ((p - 1) * p) + b^2 / (p * (p + 1)).

Step 2: Bring both fractions over the common denominator p * (p - 1) * (p + 1):
E = ((p + 1) * a^2 + (p - 1) * b^2) / (p * (p - 1) * (p + 1)).

Step 3: Notice that the two weights (p + 1) and (p - 1) in the numerator add up to (p + 1) + (p - 1) = 2 * p. Multiply both the numerator and the denominator by 2 * p:
2 * p * ((p + 1) * a^2 + (p - 1) * b^2) = ((p + 1) + (p - 1)) * ((p + 1) * a^2 + (p - 1) * b^2).

Step 4: Expand the brackets on the right side and group the cross terms by adding and subtracting 2 * (p + 1) * (p - 1) * a * b:
((p + 1)^2 * a^2 + 2 * (p + 1) * (p - 1) * a * b + (p - 1)^2 * b^2) + (p + 1) * (p - 1) * (a^2 - 2 * a * b + b^2)
= ((p + 1) * a + (p - 1) * b)^2 + (p - 1) * (p + 1) * (b - a)^2.

Step 5: Now divide both parts by the full denominator 2 * p^2 * (p - 1) * (p + 1). In the second part, the factor (p - 1) * (p + 1) cancels from top and bottom, leaving the exact identity
a^2 / ((p - 1) * p) + b^2 / (p * (p + 1)) = (b - a)^2 / (2 * p^2) + ((p + 1) * a + (p - 1) * b)^2 / (2 * p^2 * (p - 1) * (p + 1)).

Step 6: For any prime p >= 2, the denominator 2 * p^2 * (p - 1) * (p + 1) is strictly positive and the square ((p + 1) * a + (p - 1) * b)^2 is >= 0. So the second fraction is always >= 0. Substituting the prime cell jump b - a = c 1 gives
E >= (c 1)^2 / (2 * p^2),
and putting in the Dirichlet anchor c 1 = -1 gives E >= 1 / (2 * p^2).

Step 7: Finally, if we take any two primes p and q with N < p, p + 2 <= q, and q <= M, the four cell indices p - 1, p, q - 1, and q are all separate numbers inside {1, ..., M}. Since every cell term in harmonicCellL2PartialSum is a square divided by n * (n + 1) and therefore >= 0, dropping all other cells gives the two-prime lower bound
harmonicCellL2PartialSum c N M >= 1 / (2 * p^2) + 1 / (2 * q^2).

In the Lean 4 code, the algebraic identity in Step 5 is proved using field_simp and ring, and the finite sum lower bounds use Finset.sum_le_sum_of_subset_of_nonneg on the two-element and four-element subsets of Finset.Icc 1 M.
