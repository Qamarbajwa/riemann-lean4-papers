In the coordinate space files, we saw that no finite sum of M harmonic fractional part functions can cancel the constant function 1 on (0, 1] because every prime p > M creates a jump inside the unpartitioned gap (0, 1 / (M + 1)). A very natural question is what this finite truncation obstruction looks like after taking the Mellin transform to the complex s plane, and how it connects to the zeros of the Riemann zeta function.

This single push formalizes the Mellin side of that contradiction and the Cauchy Schwarz lower bound for off line zeros. When we take the Mellin transform of the Nyman residual E_M(x), we get the quotient (1 - zeta(s) * D_M(s)) / s, where D_M(s) is a finite Dirichlet polynomial. Because a finite sum D_M(s) has no poles anywhere in the complex plane, it can never cancel a zero of zeta(s). Furthermore, if zeta(s_0) = 0 at a point s_0 with real part sigma_0 > 1/2, the test function x^(s_0 - 1) belongs to L2(0, 1) with squared norm 1 / (2 * sigma_0 - 1), and Cauchy Schwarz traps the L2 residual energy above (2 * sigma_0 - 1) / |s_0|^2 > 0 for every finite M.

The step by step maths behind how this works is very simple:

Step 1: Let M[E_M](s) = (1 - zeta(s) * D_M(s)) / s be the Mellin transform quotient at a complex point s = sigma_0 + i * t_0 with s != 0.

Step 2: Suppose s_0 is any zero of the Riemann zeta function, so zeta(s_0) = 0. Since D_M(s_0) is a finite sum of M complex numbers, multiplying by zero gives
zeta(s_0) * D_M(s_0) = 0 * D_M(s_0) = 0.

Step 3: Putting zeta(s_0) * D_M(s_0) = 0 into the Mellin quotient gives
M[E_M](s_0) = (1 - 0) / s_0 = 1 / s_0.
Since s_0 != 0, we have 1 / s_0 != 0 and |M[E_M](s_0)|^2 = 1 / |s_0|^2 > 0. This immediately gives the Mellin contradiction against finite closure: if E_M(x) were zero almost everywhere on (0, 1], its Mellin transform at s_0 would have to be 0, which contradicts 1 / s_0 != 0.

Step 4: Now suppose the zero s_0 lies to the right of the critical line, so its real part satisfies sigma_0 > 1/2. On the interval (0, 1], the squared modulus of the Mellin kernel x^(s_0 - 1) is x^(2 * sigma_0 - 2). Integrating this from 0 to 1 gives
integral_0^1 x^(2 * sigma_0 - 2) dx = 1 / (2 * sigma_0 - 1),
which is finite and strictly positive because 2 * sigma_0 - 1 > 0.

Step 5: Apply the Cauchy Schwarz inequality to the Mellin integral M[E_M](s_0) = integral_0^1 E_M(x) * x^(s_0 - 1) dx:
|M[E_M](s_0)|^2 <= (integral_0^1 |E_M(x)|^2 dx) * (integral_0^1 x^(2 * sigma_0 - 2) dx)
= ||E_M||_L2^2 * (1 / (2 * sigma_0 - 1)).

Step 6: Substitute |M[E_M](s_0)|^2 = 1 / |s_0|^2 from Step 3 and multiply both sides by (2 * sigma_0 - 1) > 0:
||E_M||_L2^2 >= (2 * sigma_0 - 1) / |s_0|^2 > 0.

Step 7: Notice that the right side (2 * sigma_0 - 1) / |s_0|^2 depends only on the zero s_0 and does not depend on M or on the coefficients c_1, ..., c_M. Therefore, by simple contraposition, if ||E_M||_L2^2 can be made smaller than any positive epsilon as M grows, no such off line zero with sigma_0 > 1/2 can exist.
