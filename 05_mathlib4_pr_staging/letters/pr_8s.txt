The step by step school maths behind how this works is very straightforward:

Step 1: Since the cutoff satisfies N >= 2, every prime p in the interval [N + 1, M] is strictly greater than 2, so p >= 3 is an odd number (it leaves remainder 1 when divided by 2).

Step 2: Suppose p and q are any two distinct primes in S. Because both p and q are odd numbers and p != q, they cannot sit right next to each other (any two distinct odd integers differ by at least 2), so either p + 2 <= q or q + 2 <= p.

Step 3: Consider the adjacent cell pairs {p - 1, p} and {q - 1, q}. Since p and q differ by at least 2, we have p < q - 1 (when p + 2 <= q) or q < p - 1 (when q + 2 <= p). Therefore, the two pairs {p - 1, p} and {q - 1, q} can never share any cell index, so they are strictly disjoint.

Step 4: Because every prime p in S satisfies N + 1 <= p <= M with N >= 2, both cell indices p - 1 and p lie inside [1, M]. Using pairwise disjointness from Step 3, the disjoint union of all pairs {p - 1, p} for p in S fits cleanly inside [1, M], so adding the nonnegative cell energies over that union is at most the partial sum up to M:
sum_{p in S} harmonicPairEnergy(c, N, p) <= harmonicCellL2PartialSum(c, N, M).

sum_{p in S} 1 / (2 * p^2) <= harmonicCellL2PartialSum(c, N, M).
